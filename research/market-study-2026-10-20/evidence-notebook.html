Industrial operations

Industrial operations: market-first opportunity study

Research date: 20 October 2026. Geography: global. Baseline: laptop, public licensed data, no factory access, footage, partner, or purchased hardware. This is desk research, not customer discovery. No organizations were contacted. Recommendations below follow the evidence scan and remain hypotheses pending interviews.

Finding

Manufacturing faults represent a credible problem, but a generic “AI detects factory faults” pitch is neither differentiated nor technically honest. Separate product defects, observable process abnormalities, and internal machine failures. A product image can reveal a misplaced part; a motion sequence can reveal a stopped mechanism; internal bearing damage normally needs relevant measurements and history. NIST's 2021 US manufacturer survey associates less reactive maintenance with lower downtime and fewer defects, but does not establish a camera product's causal savings or global market size. NIST, 2021

The strongest resource-compatible industrial candidate is a factory product-fault evidence and review system for short-run PCB assembly. It should detect selected visible manufacturing faults and accelerate human disposition, rather than claim to replace industrial AOI. A machine-motion fault system is a conditional alternative with weaker accessible validation. Neither is a validated business yet.

Candidate A: Factory Fault Evidence Desk

Job: A quality engineer reviews a new product batch, sees the suspicious region against an approved reference, groups repeated faults, records disposition, and exports a traceable evidence packet.

MVP sequence: upload a known board family; OpenCV aligns it to a reference, rejects blurred or badly registered inputs, computes image differences or invokes a lightweight anomaly model, and produces fault regions. A bounded workflow checks registration, examines crops at higher resolution, compares similar earlier events, then requests review when evidence is insufficient. Human decisions feed a versioned defect library. No automatic line stop or root-cause claim.

Substantive OpenCV: feature registration/homography; illumination normalization; contour and connected-component analysis; ROI crop/mask generation; blur and alignment checks; image overlay and measurable localization. ML is optional. Test a simple reference-difference baseline first. If a learned model is used, keep normal-image training separate from defective held-out evaluation.

Day-one technical gate: no dataset sample, reference alignment pipeline, pretrained anomaly model or inference runtime has been tested in this study. VisA PCB membership does not guarantee a stable golden-reference comparison: normal pose, registration, lighting and component appearance variation may overwhelm defect differences. Download and inspect one subset before committing. On at least 50 held-out normal and 50 defective images, use a predeclared screening gate of at least 80% defect recall, at most ten false alerts per 100 normal images, and at least 90% image acceptance, with thresholds chosen using separate validation data. These are proposed demo viability thresholds, not industrial quality requirements. Report uncertainty from the small sample. If alignment fails, test one existing anomaly method within a fixed half-day budget, recording model/code licenses and laptop latency. If neither passes, drop autonomous fault detection from the promise or reject the concept; do not spend the remaining deadline polishing an unsupported detector.

Meaningful AWS: S3 holds submitted samples and evidence; an EC2-hosted container runs the inspection pipeline and stores case decisions; the interface retrieves persisted cases and compares repeat defects. These are architecture proposals, not existing deployment claims. Use an account with authorized credits/billing. No spend or account setup occurred in this study.

Data: Amazon's VisA author repository describes 10,821 images across 12 objects including four PCB subsets, image/pixel labels, and a direct S3 download. Data license is CC BY 4.0, independently listed in the AWS Open Data registry. Use one PCB subset first; retain attribution and record transformations. The images demonstrate visible product defects; they are not factory videos, failure histories or proof of any named manufacturer's process. Authors, 2022, AWS registry

Evaluation: report recall and precision at a specified threshold, false alerts per 100 normal images, accepted-image coverage, registration failure rate, latency on the actual machine, and localization overlap where meaningful. Plot the precision/recall tradeoff. Report abstentions separately. Use a separately generated lighting/blur perturbation set as a stress test, not as independent factory validation. A scripted replay is explicitly labeled replay.

Failure cases: reflections, unseen PCB revisions, hidden solder joints, tiny faults below resolution, perspective changes, threshold changes across variants and false alarms caused by normal component appearance. A registration failure must not become a confident defect. No claim that an image proves electrical functionality or IPC acceptance. Jabil's published capability list includes optical and X-ray inspection and electrical tests, illustrating why RGB inspection alone is incomplete. Jabil

Candidate B: machine-motion fault witness, conditional

If “factory faults” specifically means machinery faults, narrow the promise to observable stopped or irregular motion in a known repeated cycle. OpenCV optical flow, ROI tracking and temporal periodicity can detect change; a state machine distinguishes planned idle, running and unconfirmed anomaly. Evidence includes pre/post frames and measured cycle deviation. A suspected stop triggers more frames and operator verification rather than a claim about the broken part.

IPAD's author repository reports 16 industrial devices and over six hours of synthetic/real video, but no explicit dataset license was visible in the reviewed README. Public availability is not reuse permission. Do not redistribute it or depend on obtaining permission within the deadline. MVTec AD has a clear CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 restriction and specifically forbids commercial use; do not assume a prize/marketing demo is noncommercial. IPAD, MVTec terms

With current resources, use self-authored simulated conveyor/press videos and explicitly state that industrial transfer is untested. That is an implementable prototype, but weaker than Candidate A's licensed real images. Continue only if the judging rubric rewards transparent simulation or suitable licensed footage becomes available. Alias/low frame rate, occlusion and planned stops are central confounders. It is not predictive maintenance.

Economics and buying friction

Illustrative single-station assumptions, not market facts or a proposed validated price: 300 flagged images/day × 22 days = 6,600 reviews/month. Saving 15 seconds/review yields 27.5 hours. At an assumed loaded labor cost of $30/hour, gross time value is $825/month. A hypothetical $200/month subscription plus $30/month infrastructure leaves $595 before onboarding, support, labeling and hardware. If only 100 reviews/day and five seconds saved, value falls to about $92/month: the same offer fails. Redeployed time is not automatically cash savings.

For movement monitoring, assume four avoidable events/month × ten minutes × $5/minute = $200 gross value, then discount by prevention effectiveness. If an existing sensor already signals instantly, detection alone adds little. Never reuse giant-plant downtime averages to justify an SMB price. Customer-specific evidence must establish event frequency, economically recoverable time and true cost.

Buying friction includes camera/lighting calibration, fixture changes, board IP approval, network access, data retention, quality sign-off, model drift, process integration, scarce faulty examples and who handles false alarms. NIST describes machine vision as one of multiple automation applications for smaller manufacturers, not a turnkey universal substitution. NIST manufacturing automation

Discovery questions and kill criteria

Ask users to show the last ten defect reviews: what took time, which faults escaped, where evidence lived, and how each decision was recorded. Ask how many flags are reviewed per shift; genuine defect fraction; repeated versus novel faults; whether false alarms or missing faults cost more; board changeover frequency; and what existing AOI exports. Ask the budget owner what measured result funds a pilot and who may approve sample images. For motion monitoring, ask why the PLC/photoelectric sensor/andon signal is insufficient and whether extra context changes response.

Stop or pivot if: authorized representative samples cannot be obtained after the hackathon; existing software handles the task adequately; the buyer cannot identify a meaningful recurring review workload; permitted processing excludes the proposed architecture; the detector requires unacceptable defect escape to save review time; simulated motion is presented as factory validation; or pilot value cannot cover deployment/support. For the hackathon, stop expanding scope if one subset cannot beat a simple baseline. No need to build a full MES.

Infrastructure, environment, energy and agriculture market screen

Research date: 20 October 2026. Scope: global opportunity search; country-specific evidence is identified and never extrapolated into global market size. Constraints: no factory, customer, specialist hardware, private footage or supplied data; six days to the stated 26 October deadline. This is desk research, not customer validation. Decision before concepts

Four recurring needs were screened: pavement maintenance evidence, sewer inspection throughput, solar maintenance triage, and crop-health diagnosis. All have established workflows and suppliers. None supports a claim that an inexpensive camera plus AI is an unserved category. Solar provides the cleanest small public dataset; roads provide the strongest multinational benchmark. Both are conditional hackathon options, not validated businesses. Sewer and generic crop diagnosis should be rejected under present constraints.

No global TAM is calculated. Installed infrastructure, crop losses and equipment downtime are not software revenue. A credible serviceable market would require counts of reachable buyers with compatible data, purchasing authority and incremental unmet need; those are unknown.

Need and evidence. Road managers need consistent condition records to select maintenance work. Buyer, user, payer. Municipal public-works directors and engineering consultants buy; pavement analysts and maintenance supervisors use; municipal operating/capital budgets or consultant project budgets pay. A plausible initial segment is consultants producing evidence for small municipal clients, because one consulting relationship can cover multiple road networks. This channel advantage is an untested hypothesis.

4. Crop-health diagnosis: reject generic consumer app

FAO's 2021 estimate that pests destroy up to 40% of crops establishes broad damage context only. It does not tell us which visible symptoms are preventable, who pays for software or how much they would pay. FAO.

Plantix already offers free photo diagnosis and treatment suggestions; its B2B page claims eight million yearly active users and 150 million images. Kindwise crop.health offers API diagnosis, with public credit pricing from €0.05 to €0.01 by volume and 100 trial credits. Cropin sells enterprise monitoring and disease warnings; it names PepsiCo, McCain and East-West Seed among customers. Those organizations verify buyer categories, not demand for this team. Plantix, B2B scale, Kindwise pricing, Cropin customers.

Farmers use diagnostics, but agribusiness, insurers, extension programs or input distributors may pay. That payer/user split needs validation. Agronomists, lab testing, weather models and field scouting are substitutes. A leaf image cannot reliably distinguish every nutrient, pest and pathogen cause.

PlantVillage has over 54,000 controlled leaf images. Its original study obtained about 99% internal test accuracy but only 31.4% on external images, demonstrating domain-shift risk rather than current performance ceilings. Original image permissions must be inspected before use; no mirror's license should substitute. Dataset authors, original experiment.

Decision: reject. Without field access, an agronomist and a specific crop/buyer workflow, a six-day classifier would offer weak differentiation against free diagnosis and cheap APIs. Neither a chatbot nor AWS hosting fixes the commercial gap.

Economics and validation boundaries

All unit economics below are assumptions to test, not market prices: at $30/hour review labor, saving four hours yields $120 per job. A $30/job price captures 25% of that gross benefit before onboarding, support, error correction and cloud costs. If only 30 minutes is saved, gross benefit falls to $15 and the example price fails. For 20 jobs/month the first assumption yields $600 monthly revenue per contractor; this is a scenario, not a forecast. Do not price based on total energy/crop/infrastructure losses.

Before building commercially: interview at least five relevant buyers, observe two actual workflows and compare against their incumbent and manual process. Ask for a bounded retrospective evaluation with representative licensed images and measured time, not hypothetical enthusiasm. Require a named payer, an integration path and a reason an existing tool is insufficient. No outreach was conducted or authorized. Hackathon success can be demonstrated in six days; purchase intent and field reliability cannot.

Decision before concepts

The best-supported pain in this workstream is getting usable, complete documents into operational systems at the first attempt. Freight operators already link shipping documents to billing, and buyers already purchase capture SDKs. However, generic document scanning is mature. A project is defensible only as a narrow evidence-completeness and recapture workflow with measurable advantage over an ordinary scanner. Second, medical image privacy quality assurance has unusually good public evaluation data and clear institutional need, but greater implementation and procurement friction. Assistive vision and warehouse safety have real need and strong incumbents; neither should become a default recommendation simply because an attractive demo is possible.

These conclusions are comparative hypotheses, not proof of product-market fit. Public evidence proves workflows and alternatives; it does not prove an unmet niche.

Whole-image sharpness alone is insufficient: a page can be sharp while its relevant field is missing. Existing SDKs may already support this through configuration, so “AI-guided recapture” is not established novelty. Insurance claim-document intake is an adjacent workflow, but this study found no customer-side claim rework rate or licensed claim-packet benchmark; do not pitch insurer savings without further validation.

Six-day MVP. Use OpenCV 5 quadrilateral detection, homography, local blur/contrast measures, region-level coverage, feature alignment and perceptual duplicate detection. A small configurable state machine asks for a better frame or missing page. AWS hosts asynchronous packet inspection, maintains submission/review state, and stores evidence with retention controls. Build a browser upload and replay demo; a camera is optional. Keep signature detection out of the first version - presence is not authenticity.

SmartDoc15 supplies 150 document videos, roughly 24,000 frames, corner annotations and CC BY 4.0 licensing. It tests capture geometry and distortions, not freight-field semantics. Dataset. Use it for geometry evaluation and create original fictional delivery forms for packet completeness. Split by document/video, never random adjacent frames. Economics and validation. Illustrative assumptions: 10,000 monthly packets, 5% avoidable rework, three minutes per rework, $20/hour labour imply $500/month gross recoverable labour. A proposed $100-150 monthly product needs to save materially more than its integration/support cost. These inputs are unmeasured, not benchmark rates. Kill if interviews find under 1% actionable rework, existing SDK configuration solves it, or recapture interrupts drivers more than it saves. Request an anonymized rejection-reason tally and observe five complete workflows before writing an ROI claim. Procurement needs app integration, offline handling and access/retention decisions.
