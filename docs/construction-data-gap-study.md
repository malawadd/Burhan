# Construction inspection: real data, local models and remaining gaps

Research date: **20 October 2026**. This document extends the [Saudi inspection study](saudi-construction-inspection-study.md) with data/model availability for **every one of its nine potentially visual families** and all ten remaining evidence categories. The [89-family checklist](saudi-construction-checklist.md) remains a research catalog, not a claim of automatic detection.

Public availability, a model demo and lawful commercial redistribution are separate questions. Dataset/model scope below comes from primary publisher repositories, institutional archives or official model cards. A source marked “not verified” is a lead, not installed functionality. No synthetic data or repeated-still video is counted as real-world evidence.

## Actual footage obtained

The strongest new source is [Tsegaye's 2026 curated bridge dataset](https://zenodo.org/records/19268973), **CC BY 4.0**, whose publisher explicitly separates raw drone bridge video, manually annotated images and inference images. Its metadata and ZIP directory were inspected directly. It contains **39 original raw MP4 members**; durations were checked from the original MP4 movie headers using byte ranges. They span approximately **12.16 to 135.79 seconds**. These are bridge inspection recordings, not Saudi building-stage inspections or independently labelled crack-video tests.

| `bd_vid_0009.mp4` | Downloaded unmodified; 380,386,528 bytes; ZIP CRC `e8285071` verified; SHA-256 `f74413de3a3997108435754b7c462f06072d2a695ca265f503afdb89f4ebcf7b`; OpenCV opens 3840×2160, 914 frames, 30.028496 FPS, **30.437755 seconds** | Longer real moving input; no independent frame/event labels verified |

Local originals and attribution are under `datasets/construction/bridge_raw/`. The [fetch script](../scripts/fetch_construction_bridge_video.py) retrieves one exact ZIP member with HTTP byte ranges, refuses a full-archive response, checks its CRC, and writes a SHA-256/provenance record. It avoids downloading the full **18,463,440,612-byte** raw-video archive. Publisher archive MD5 is recorded as publisher metadata, **not** represented as locally verified when the full archive was not downloaded.

The implementation agent visually checked the first clip's original frame around three seconds: actual concrete bridge underside, steel girders and a very thin hairline crack. Resizing a 4K frame to the model's 640-pixel input can erase such a feature; original scene visibility is not proof that the model will detect it. Its actual predictions and failures belong in the execution report.

```powershell
.venv\Scripts\python.exe -X utf8 scripts\fetch_construction_bridge_video.py --member bd_vid_0009.mp4
```

The earlier [2025 bridge inference release](https://zenodo.org/records/17477702) also contains actual-video-derived outputs but already has model overlays. It is unsuitable as independent visual truth and can bias a fresh detector. The raw 2026 release is preferred. Human image annotations in a separate archive do not automatically supply video event labels.

The [construction implementation report](construction-vision.md) additionally records an actual **5.24-second CC0 Nigerian building-site clip** and a **244.55-second public-domain NIOSH live-action interview**. The interview is usable original footage but is not representative continuous site surveillance or an inspection test. The real video sources have different domains and evidence value; duration alone does not validate them.

## Matrix for all nine potentially visual catalog families

“Visual” means that a camera may show a candidate condition under suitable capture. It does not mean the installed software detects that condition, can see hidden work, or verifies code acceptance.

|---|---|---|---|---|

Waterproofing appears in other catalog evidence categories, not in the nine `video_visual` rows. Visible membrane appearance, wet spots or efflorescence can support review, but **no verified public video dataset/model here establishes membrane watertightness**. An actual flood/leak/integrity test, system identification and calibrated details remain necessary. A defect model trained to see a wet spot must not report “waterproofing passed.”

The implementation agent downloaded and checked original ONNX files rather than executing an arbitrary downloaded Python/pickle model:

| Surface crack | [beaunix, revision 9442007](https://huggingface.co/beaunix/smart-crack-vision/tree/9442007ef9c1003880baa5796d253608691b37c4) | SHA-256 `4b789658646d790079d3e78d9d3ed475030d740a4b8412aff52248189a114cbd`; box/mask contract checked | Author and metadata AGPL-3.0; training-distributor Public Domain statement does not itself settle every upstream asset right |

The [measured report](construction-vision.md) establishes a **compatibility diagnostic**, not independent Saudi site accuracy: PPE recall **71.30%** on 169 public images; crack box recall **68.24%** on 112 public images. Training overlap is unknown. CPU-to-GPU optimization preserved predictions. On five pre-labelled frames of genuine site video, the original square-stretch preprocessing missed the primary worker's bare head in **all five**, and matched no primary-worker person boxes. An aspect-preserving letterbox alternative was selected by a predefined tie-break after both options produced identical macro F1 on 170 separate VALID images (all already square); test/video labels did not select the geometry. Replaying the already-seen video frames improved person matches to **3/5** and bare-head matches to **1/5**. Current bare-head recall is therefore still only **20% in this tiny, correlated, reused diagnostic**, with four of five missed. It is not a fresh independent evaluation or dependable PPE safety monitoring. The public image counts stayed unchanged; no extra data/model lead in this study fixes the remaining measured misses by assertion.

Raw bridge execution also exposed false crack candidates on steel beam joints. An explicit operator-selected concrete ROI did not establish a fix: in its actual midpoint frame, a prominent thin horizontal crack-like feature remained unboxed while a low-score proposal appeared near a panel/joint boundary. The 34 ROI proposals across 30 frames are not 34 verified concrete cracks; no material-specific per-frame truth was available. Actual source-paced raw 4K processing also failed a 10 FPS request, achieving about 3 FPS with 267 source frames skipped. These failures remain visible in the [implementation report](construction-vision.md); lower-resolution source proxies, if used, are separately identified and cannot inherit raw-video accuracy by assertion.

Consequently, the honest product claim is “local experimental visual observations with documented gaps.” Dependable safety or code inspection requires substantially better independent real-video validation before deployment. Model availability does not make all nine families implemented; the app still exposes unsupported inspections as unassessed.

## Data collection needed for a stronger product

Prioritize actual site videos before adding more output labels. Capture independently labelled normal/defective cases for each proposed class, including camera motion, blur, dust, low light, occlusion, distance and hard negatives. Split by project and original recording, hold out Saudi sites, freeze thresholds before testing, and score missed events and false alerts per hour. Preserve original video timing and identify every sampled frame and drop.

For rebar and formwork, pair visible footage with measured layout/cover and signed pre-pour records. For concrete, pair surface labels with qualified classifications and relevant physical tests. For waterproofing/MEP/fire, connect each video event to actual test equipment/readings and the approved system. For all dimensions, retain the scale/calibration and measurement error.

These are missing real inputs, not features that can be completed by replaying stock footage or choosing a broader model name. The datasets above create research starting points and actual local video evidence; none establishes complete automated Saudi construction inspection.
